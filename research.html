The physics of extremes

Our work covers weather and climate dynamics and the hydrological cycle, with an emphasis on extreme precipitation, hurricanes, and other extremes in coastal regions and beyond. We combine observations with climate model simulations to understand the mechanisms behind these events, their response to climate change, and their predictability.

Extreme precipitation and the hydrological cycle

Heavy rainfall sits at the far end of the hydrological cycle. We study how moisture supply, the atmospheric circulation, and storm dynamics combine to produce extreme precipitation, and how its intensity, frequency, and seasonality change as the climate warms.

Questions

  1. What physical processes set the intensity and frequency of extreme precipitation?
  2. How do thermodynamic (moisture) and dynamic (circulation) changes each contribute to future precipitation change?
  3. Why do precipitation responses differ between seasons and regions?

    Coastal weather extremes

    Coastal regions face some of the most damaging weather on Earth. We investigate the heavy rainfall, hurricanes, and other high-impact weather that strike the coast: the low-level jets, ocean conditions, and large-scale environments that feed them, and how these may change in a warmer climate.

    Questions

    1. How do low-level jets and onshore moisture transport set up extreme rainfall along the Gulf Coast?
    2. How do ocean conditions and the large-scale environment shape hurricanes and other coastal extremes?
    3. How will coastal weather extremes respond to climate change, and how predictable are they?

      Circulation, oceans, and external forcing

      Extremes are organized by the large-scale flow. We examine how the atmospheric general circulation, ocean conditions, and external forcing, such as rising CO₂, shape weather and climate extremes and their variability on regional and global scales.

      Questions

      1. How do storm tracks and eddy moisture transport respond to warming?
      2. What is the direct radiative effect of CO₂ on regional precipitation, apart from ocean warming?
      3. How do ocean conditions modulate regional extremes?

        Climate change and predictability

        Separating the forced response from internal variability is central to interpreting observed trends and projecting the future. Combining observations with climate model simulations, we evaluate how well models capture historical changes and what that means for the predictability of extremes.

        Questions

        1. Are observed precipitation trends consistent with climate model simulations?
        2. How much of an observed trend is forced, and how much is atmospheric internal variability?
        3. What sets the predictability of extremes, from seasons to decades?
