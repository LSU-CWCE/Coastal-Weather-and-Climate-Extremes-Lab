About the lab

Why extremes

We want to understand the physical mechanisms behind weather and climate extremes, how they respond to a changing climate, and how predictable they are.

Our research covers weather and climate dynamics and the hydrological cycle, with an emphasis on extreme precipitation, hurricanes, and other extremes in coastal regions and beyond.

We look at how the atmospheric general circulation, ocean conditions, and external forcing shape these events and their variability on regional and global scales, using observations and climate model simulations.

How we work

From records to mechanisms

Observations show what has changed. Model experiments and moisture budgets explain why, and together they tell us what to expect.

Observations

Observational records of heavy rainfall, storms, and the large-scale circulation show how extremes have changed.

Model experiments

Climate model simulations and targeted experiments isolate the roles of individual forcings and processes.

Moisture budgets

Moisture budget analysis separates the thermodynamic and dynamic processes behind extreme precipitation.

Change and predictability

We assess how high-impact events respond to climate change and how predictable they are.
